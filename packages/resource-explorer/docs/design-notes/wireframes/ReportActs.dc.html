Record · report · the three acts

Acting on what was true

The three acts on a list operate on a live selection. On a report the rows are frozen, so the acts mean something different and must say so: they act on the snapshot, and what they create points back at the record rather than at a query. That is the difference between a work item someone can still read next month and one whose numbers have quietly moved underneath it.

from dependency_analysis, run 2026-09-13 09:14 · written by dwolfson, 09:16 · a snapshot, not a query
The whole report · 32 rows · as recorded 2026-09-13
or pick rows to act on some of them

The whole report is the default, where a list defaults to nothing. The rows were already chosen once — that is what saving them was. Picking is for the person triaging a report they were handed, not for the person who wrote it.

“Someone must.”

The detail carries the same line, and the RFA points at the record — not at a re-derived list.

So the person who receives it opens exactly what the person who raised it was looking at. That is the whole reason the record exists; an RFA that re-queried would make it decorative.

Not blocked — carried. The acts stay live, and what they create says what it was made from: … as recorded in “High advisories with a fix”, whose evidence has since moved — cve_scan re-ran 09-12.

Flag, do not invalidate — the same rule the enrichment judgements follow, one layer out. Someone may have perfectly good reasons to act on last week's list; what they may not do is act on it without the work item knowing. A blocked button would just send them to copy the rows by hand.

And the record learns it was used
Used · added to work list “Dependencies to fix before 6.2” · 09-14 08:12 · dwolfson
Used · cited in the journal · 09-14 08:20 · dwolfson

A record's uses are a separate append-only list attached to it, not an edit of it. The content stays frozen — that is what makes it a record — while what it caused accumulates beside it.

Which is the answer to the fair question of why a record should exist at all rather than a download: it is traceable in both directions. From a work item you can reach what was true when it was raised; from the record you can see what it caused. A CSV in someone's downloads folder does neither.

Four things these acts must not do. Re-derive the list (the record becomes decorative the moment they do). Mutate the record (uses attach; content is frozen). Name a slug where a list has a name — now in “Dependencies to fix before 6.2”, as promotion learned. Or appear at all to an anonymous viewer without saying why: Sign in to act on a report — a work item needs someone who raised it. Gated, not hidden.

Still not drawn, and named: the write a correction act. The sentence exists on an out-of-date record and there is no button, which makes it a dead end — a record that can say it is wrong and offers no way to say what is right. It is the next small round, not part of this one.
