Say which population, and whose caveat

All three are one habit: a row composed from several analyses over several populations, written as though it came from one.

Point 5 · the two sentences that cannot both be true
24 components recovered — 4 of 87 components reviewed (4 accepted); 0 of 12 blueprints reviewed · architecture_doc_lens ran and found nothing. · 87 candidate components, none documented
This analysis ran and found nothing — a measured zero, not a gap in coverage.

Both are true, and the row makes them contradict. The provenance names three analyses. The 24 is architecture_recovery's. The nothing is architecture_doc_lens's — and the answer line already says so, attributed, in the middle of itself. Then the caveat says it again as “This analysis”, unattributed, directly under a number from a different one.

The rule, and it is the same one #88 just applied to rationale-as-caveat: a caveat on a multi-analysis row names its analysis, or it does not render. This has no referent where there are three.

Point 4 · 24 recovered from what?

Three populations, and the line mixes them without naming any: 24 is the projection at depth 1, 87 is every recovered component, and 12 is the candidate blueprints. All three are legitimate and none is interchangeable, so every count carries its population — at depth 1, recovered, candidate — and no sentence holds two populations without the word of between them.

And why we think each is what it is

A component's type came from a detector, with a confidence and a proposed_by list that the row never shows. It should: a console entry point, a compose service, two detectors agree. Those are the words that let someone accept or reject without opening the evidence table — and the branch tree is where they belong.

Completeness, stated

“We are not showing complete lists” is fair, and the honest fix is not always to show all of them — it is to say what is shown and what is not: 24 of 87 shown at this depth · 63 nested below · all 87 ›. The tree already carries this at the branch; the answer row does not.

Point 6 · what a public interface is, and the rung that is missing

interface_surface already has a ladder — its own comment says “openapi.yaml is 'specified'; a fastapi dependency is only 'implied'”. Two rungs, and the truth is almost always on the one between them.

rungwhat it meansevidence
declared a contract committed in the repository, or an entry point the packaging declares openapi.yaml, .proto, [project.scripts]
implemented · missing the interface exists in the code, whether or not anything documents it — routes, commands, exported operations FastAPI route decorators, click commands, the CLI's own entry function
implied something in the dependency list suggests an interface and nothing confirms it depends on click, depends on fastapi
cli — implied. Depends on click — suggests a cli, but nothing in the repo specifies one.

That sentence is wrong on this repository, and the fact that refutes it is already in the codebase four times over. [project.scripts] is read by repo_role.py, by distribution_parser.py — whose own docstring calls it “the CLI entry point as a fact” — by an architecture-recovery rule, and by deployment_evidence.py, which shipped yesterday and calls a console entry point the strongest signal. interface_surface consults none of them.

Which is the vendored-rule defect again, one analytic over: a fact known by one walk and not read by another, producing a confident sentence that the repository itself contradicts. The guard test for walks exists because of the last one.
