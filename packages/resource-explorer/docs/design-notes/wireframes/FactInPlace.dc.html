The numbers come first, the rows come next

The disclosure shows the measurements — six rows, each a name and a value — and each one that has rows behind it says so with its own count. 965 file names are not the fact behind 965 source files; the number is, and the names are one step further for the person who wants them.

That is what the owner could not find: not a list, a table of six.

Rows still go to the rail, and now they earn it

the 965 files › opens the members rail exactly as it does today, with its groups, its scope chips and its footer. A column of mono file names is the one thing 290px suits.

The rail stops being where you go to find out what a number means, and becomes where you go to see the things it counted. Counts open what they counted — one layer down, not one pane over.

Every state keeps its sentence

A metrics-only analysis says records measurements, not members where the row's opens cell would be — which is the fix that just shipped, moved one surface out so the promise is never made.

An unset renders not recorded, never a zero. A zero stays a zero. Both already ruled; both apply here unchanged.

What this deletes. Dashboard · by question exists to host this disclosure; once the disclosure is on the row, that view is the answer sentence rendered a second time for no remaining reason. Its anchor nav, its N questions at this stage summary line and its disagreement blocks all have homes: the summary on the Questions header, which already counts answered and loading; the disagreements under the question they dispute, where one of the two implementations already puts them.
