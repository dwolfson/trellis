What a component verdict is about

A verdict is about the component — the thing at that path — not about the proposal that surfaced it. It stays keyed by scope_locator, with no schema change. Four things have to change because of that, and one word has to go.

First, the word: three things are called “perspective”

This is why the question was unanswerable. Blueprint verdicts key on the second row; the diagram's preference keys on the first; the chrome's filter is the third and is unrelated to both. One word, three axes — and “does a verdict apply across perspectives?” has a different answer for each.

Perspective keeps only its chrome meaning, because that is the one users already hold. The defect under the defect is this project's own favourite kind: a name that means two things, one walk away from another that reads it.

The row · both proposals shown, one verdict recorded

Today the card's type, confidence and reading come from max(comp_rows, key=surveyed_at) — whichever step wrote last. So a curator can rule on a card whose attributes came from the other extractor than the drawing they clicked from, and nothing says so.

One verdict, recorded against the path. The trail notes which proposals stood when it was made, so a verdict read next year says what was on screen.

And the agreement is the strongest signal in the data. latest wins throws away the fact that two unrelated methods — one reading manifests and Dockerfiles, one reading twenty-four months of co-change — landed on the same path. Nothing else in the recovery is evidence of that quality, and it is the answer to “why do we think this is which kind”. So the sort follows: agreement outranks a single high confidence. Two detectors at 75% and 45% is a better bet than one at 90%.

When one extractor withdraws · flag, do not invalidate

This asymmetry is already shipped: _withdraw_vacated lets a step withdraw only the scopes it wrote, while verdicts are path-keyed. So coupling can withdraw its proposal on a path that still carries a verdict earned from detect's — and today that reads as nothing at all.

Not “still accepted” and not “unaccepted”: accepted, and no longer proposed by one of the extractors that proposed it.
why the verdict stands

The thing at that path either exists or does not, and a re-run of a detector is not new information about that. But the person who accepted it deserves to know the ground moved — which is the perishability treatment the enrichment judgements already have, third time out.

The coverage sentence holds two identity regimes
4 of 87 components reviewed (4 accepted); 0 of 12 blueprints reviewed
Components are keyed by path; blueprints by perspective::cluster_name. Both halves are right and the sentence hides that they count different kinds of thing.
4 of 87 component paths reviewed · 0 of 12 clusters in the logical reading reviewed
A cluster only exists within a reading; a component exists whatever you were reading when you found it. The whole ruling, said where it is load-bearing.

And the diagram states its own scope beside the caption it already has — found by coupling · detect also on file › — because under this ruling a verdict earned on one drawing styles nodes on the other. _DIAGRAM_PERSPECTIVE_PREFERENCE's coupling-first order stays; it is a reasonable default and nothing better is known. What stays recorded as deliberate is that the two are never merged.

Deliberately not settled

Where two extractors propose components over the same scope at different granularities — one composing the other — curation-lenses-design.md §4.4's overlap object is needed, with its three resolutions: same component, nested, or distinct per reading. This ruling handles the same-path case, which is the common one. Nested proposals are a round of their own.
